\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We held seven neural MTPPs to both sides of the prediction--simulation
tension on three Coinbase assets at production rates, with failures
counted as results. The uncapped \sppp{}
decoder is not a reliable simulator: it fails verified calibration on 6
of 9 checkpoints (all three on SOL), and a faithful port of the published
configuration fails 3 of 9 with the same signatures. \ssppp{}, differing only in the output heads, achieves the best SOL NLL
and accuracy (PCT-LSTM has lower MAE), leads on ETH, ties the best BTC
marks, verifies calibration on all nine checkpoints, and leads five of
twelve stylized facts, competitive on the rest.

First, closed-loop reliability tracked the intensity head, not training
quality: the uncapped head fails reproducibly with near-critical
rescaling sensitivity; the bounded head's calibration was a smooth
root-find that always verified.
Second, the bound was nearly free: it did not reduce predictive
performance in our experiments, trains at scan speed, and buys exact
thinning far above real time.

The evidence has limits: three streams from one crypto venue, seven days
each; realism is scored on signed order-flow statistics, not price paths.
The guarantee is a bounded rate, not realism: the LSTM is stable yet
near-Poisson, \ssppp{} leaves a Fano gap, \nhp{} keeps the BTC timing
lead, and head-component attribution awaits ablations. Agent-conditioned marks cannot violate the ceiling (the mark
head is rate-neutral at a fixed state); this extension and
\citet{chang2024probabilistic}'s queries are future work.
