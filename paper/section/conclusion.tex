\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We held six neural MTPPs to both sides of the prediction--simulation
tension on three Coinbase assets at production rates ($24$--$48$
events/s), with failures counted as results. The uncapped \sppp{}
decoder is not a reliable simulator: it fails verified calibration on 6
of 9 checkpoints (all three on SOL among them); the three that pass are
erratic across seeds. \ssppp{}, differing only in the output heads,
achieves the best SOL NLL and type accuracy (PCT-LSTM has lower MAE
there), leads on ETH, ties the strongest baseline on BTC marks, verifies
calibration on all nine checkpoints, and has the lowest error on five of
twelve stylized-fact metrics, competitive on the rest.

First, closed-loop reliability tracked the intensity head, not training
quality: the uncapped head fails reproducibly, with rescaling sensitivity
consistent with near-critical behavior; the bounded head's calibration was
a smooth root-find that verified every time.
Second, the bound was nearly free: it did not reduce predictive
performance in our experiments, trains at scan speed, and buys exact
thinning far above real time.

The evidence has limits: three streams from one crypto venue, seven days
each; the simulator emits types and times, so realism is scored on signed
order-flow statistics, not price paths. The guarantee is a
bounded rate, not realism: the LSTM is stable yet near-Poisson, \ssppp{}
leaves a Fano dispersion gap, \nhp{} keeps the BTC timing lead, and
head-component attribution awaits ablations. Because the mark head is rate-neutral at a fixed state,
conditioning it on an agent's resting quotes cannot violate the ceiling;
this world-model extension, and fill-probability queries
\citep{chang2024probabilistic}, are future work.
