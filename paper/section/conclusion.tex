\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We held seven neural MTPPs to both sides of the prediction--simulation
tension on three Coinbase assets at production rates; failures count as
results. The uncapped \sppp{}
decoder is not a reliable simulator: it fails verified calibration on 6
of 9 checkpoints (all three on SOL), and a faithful published-configuration
port fails 3 of 9 with the same signatures. \ssppp{}, differing only in the output heads, achieves the best SOL NLL
and accuracy (PCT-LSTM has lower MAE), leads on ETH, ties the best BTC
marks, verifies calibration on all nine checkpoints, and leads five of
twelve stylized facts, competitive on the rest.

First, closed-loop reliability tracked the head, not training quality:
the uncapped head fails reproducibly with near-critical rescaling
sensitivity; the bounded head's calibration was a smooth root-find that
always verified.
Second, the bound was nearly free: predictive performance held, training
runs at scan speed, and exact thinning runs far above real time.

Limits: three streams from one crypto venue; realism is scored on signed
order-flow statistics, not price paths.
The guarantee is boundedness, not realism: the LSTM is stable yet
near-Poisson, \ssppp{} leaves a Fano gap, \nhp{} keeps the BTC timing
lead, and component attribution awaits ablations. Agent-conditioned marks
cannot violate the ceiling; this and \citet{chang2024probabilistic}'s
queries are open.
